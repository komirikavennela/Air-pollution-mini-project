1. Introduction

Air pollution is the presence of harmful substances in the air at levels that can damage human health, the environment and the climate. Common sources include vehicle emissions, industrial activity, agricultural practices, burning of waste and natural events such as dust storms. Exposure is linked to respiratory illness, cardiovascular disease and wider ecosystem damage.

During the Community Service Project, awareness campaigns, school workshops and community meetings were conducted in Gudipallipadu. This mini project builds on that work. It studies how aware residents are of air pollution, what health effects they report, and what measures they suggest, using a questionnaire-based survey of 100 residents.

2. Objectives

  • To assess the level of awareness about air pollution among residents.
  • To identify the health effects of air pollution reported by residents.
  • To suggest measures to mitigate air pollution in the community.

6. Findings

  • Awareness: 75% of residents know the causes and effects of air pollution, while 25% do not.
  • Health effects: 40% report respiratory problems and 20% report cardiovascular problems.
  • Livelihood: Agriculture (50%) and daily wage labour (30%) are the main sources of income, so residents spend long hours outdoors.
  • Economic impact: 20% of residents report economic losses due to air pollution.
  • Suggested measures: Residents suggested promoting clean energy, improving waste management practices and increasing green cover.

7. Discussion

Although three out of four residents are aware of air pollution, a large share still report health problems. This suggests that awareness alone is not enough; practical measures are needed to reduce exposure at the source. Since many residents work in agriculture and as daily wage labourers, they are exposed to dust, smoke and burning of waste for long periods. The 25% of residents who are not aware of the causes and effects show that awareness programmes should continue and reach every household.

8. Recommendations

Promote clean energy

Encourage solar, wind and biogas to reduce dependence on fossil fuels.

Improve waste management

Segregation, recycling and composting to reduce waste burning.

Increase green cover

Plant trees and develop green spaces to absorb pollutants.

Raise awareness

Continue campaigns, school programmes and community meetings.

Monitor air quality

Set up air quality monitoring to track pollutant levels and guide decisions.

9. Conclusion, Limitations and Future Scope

Conclusion. The mini project shows that air pollution has a significant impact on health and environment in Gudipallipadu. Awareness is fairly high (75%), but 40% of residents still report health problems and 20% report economic losses. Promoting clean energy, improving waste management and increasing green cover can help reduce air pollution, along with continued awareness programmes.

Limitations. The study was limited to Gudipallipadu and a sample of 100 residents, and the results are based on self-reported responses. It may not be representative of other areas.

Future scope. Further studies can assess the impact of air pollution on specific health outcomes, evaluate the effectiveness of awareness programmes, and develop strategies to promote clean energy and better waste management.
